# REQUIREMENTS.md — Binding Standards under Stoa (Template)

> **Adopter notes.** This template lists thirteen binding standards numbered R1 through R13. Each is grounded in an external literature; you are expected to retain that grounding in your populated version. Adapt the wording of each standard to your discipline; do *not* drop standards or weaken them, because each is load-bearing to the framework's design. Italicised paragraphs are guidance and should be deleted from the final populated file. Reference `BIBLIOGRAPHY.md` for the full citations.

These are the standards every output produced under Stoa must satisfy before it can leave any chat in this project. Reviewer-facing deliverables and award-committee-facing deliverables are governed by the same bar; there is no relaxed mode. Each standard is grounded in named, dated literature listed in `BIBLIOGRAPHY.md`. The grounding is not decorative: a standard that loses its citation anchor loses its load-bearing role and must be rewritten or removed in the next revision pass.

The standards are numbered R1 through R13 for stable reference across all artefacts and chats. Per-paper `CONTEXT.md` files cite R-numbers directly when locking decisions; the Paper Evaluation Chamber audit verdict cites R-numbers explicitly when assessing compliance.

## R1 — Doctoral-grade ambition with [PROGRAMME-SPECIFIC] bar

Every draft is engineered to be simultaneously reviewer-facing for top-tier journals and award-committee-facing for [SPECIFY THE DISCIPLINARY SOCIETIES OR AWARDS RELEVANT TO YOUR PROGRAMME]. The decision to *target* a specific award lives in `STATE.md`; the requirement to *write to that bar* applies universally and from the first draft.

The bar is grounded in the doctoral-writing pedagogy literature on what distinguishes acceptable from exceptional scholarly work. Sword (2012) establishes that the difference between competent and award-grade prose is detectable at sentence level and is the product of deliberate craft. Belcher (2019) demonstrates that the same paper drafted with and without explicit attention to the highest-tier reviewer's expectations produces materially different reviewer responses. R1 codifies the upper-end expectation of both literatures into a non-negotiable design constraint.

## R2 — Theoretical originality and contribution clarity

Every paper must articulate, in the introduction and revisited in the discussion, (i) the gap in the existing literature it closes, (ii) the theoretical mechanism it proposes or refines, and (iii) the boundary conditions of its claims. Replicative or descriptive contributions are insufficient for the targeted journals. A "so what" paragraph is mandatory in the discussion section.

The standard is grounded in the canonical literature on theoretical contribution. Whetten (1989) establishes the four building blocks of a theoretical contribution — what, how, why, and the *who, where, when* of boundary conditions. Sutton and Staw (1995) and Weick (1995) clarify what theory *is not*: not a list of references, not a model diagram, not a hypothesis statement, not an empirical regularity. Corley and Gioia (2011) extend the conversation to ask what kinds of theoretical contribution are plausibly novel in a mature field.

## R3 — Methodological transparency and reproducibility

Methods sections must specify data provenance, sampling logic, coding protocols, analytical procedures, and reproducibility provisions in sufficient detail that an independent researcher could replicate the analysis from the paper alone. *Adapt this paragraph to your domain: name the discipline-specific transparency conventions, the IRB or ethics-committee provisions, the open-science commitments your discipline observes, and any methodology-specific transparency requirements (DSR provisions, ethnographic positionality statements, statistical pre-registration, computational reproducibility commitments).*

The standard is grounded in the methodological transparency literature. Aguinis, Ramani, and Alabduljader (2018) establish the field-level expectation that quantitative and qualitative work alike report enough procedural detail for replicability. Aguinis and Solarino (2019) extend the standard to qualitative research with a twelve-criterion framework. Bettis, Ethiraj, Gambardella, Helfat, and Mitchell (2016) make the case that without methodological transparency, cumulative knowledge in management research is unattainable.

## R4 — Verifiable, current sourcing

Every empirical or normative claim is anchored to a verifiable source. Your default citation horizon for time-sensitive claims is the most recent five years; older works are admissible only when they are foundational or canonical. Grey literature is admissible where it is the authoritative source, but must never substitute for peer-reviewed scholarship where peer-reviewed scholarship exists. URLs, DOIs, access dates, and version numbers are mandatory for grey-literature citations.

The standard is grounded in the integrity literature on scholarly sourcing. The Committee on Publication Ethics (2019) Core Practices specify that citations must be traceable, accurate, and contextually faithful to the cited work; fabricated, mis-attributed, or "list" citations are absolute disqualifications. The grey-literature provisions follow the principle in Booth, Colomb, Williams, Bizup, and Fitzgerald (2016) that authority is determined by the proximity of the source to the underlying evidence.

## R5 — [CITATION FORMAT] throughout

All in-text citations and reference lists conform to [APA 7 / Chicago / Harvard / MLA / OSCOLA / journal-specific convention — choose one and adapt the rest of this paragraph]. Hanging indents, italicisation, capitalisation, and ordering are checked at the evaluation gate, not at submission. Reference management is performed in a tool of the author's choice.

The choice is a deliberate trade-off: a single citation standard programme-wide is materially easier to maintain and to audit for cross-paper coherence (R12) than mixed conventions. The 2020 edition of APA is the current standard at the time of writing for the management research conventions this template was developed against; adopters in disciplines that prefer Chicago, OSCOLA, or other conventions should select accordingly.

## R6 — Elegant academic prose, [REGISTER] register

Drafts are written in continuous, well-argued prose. Bullet-point summaries are avoided in the body of the paper unless the journal explicitly requires them; structured lists are admissible only in tables, appendices, or methodological boilerplate. The register is professional, [REGISTER — for example: consultative, doctoral-grade, never colloquial; or alternatively scientific, plain-language, technically precise — match to your discipline]. Sentence-level craft — varied length, controlled cadence, precise diction — is part of the bar.

The standard is grounded in the academic-style literature. Sword (2012) demonstrates that high-impact academic prose is *stylish* in a specific, learnable sense. Williams and Bizup (2017) supply the operational rules. Bem (2003) supplies the empirical-paper-specific style guidance.

## R7 — Voice consistency and persona discipline

Voice is governed by two stacked layers. The discipline-specific drafting bundle (if installed) sets paper-level drafting mechanics. The Academic Vern persona — substance adopted, closing humour permanently disabled — sets the evidentiary standard at the sentence level: every claim requires evidence; every approach needs citations; comparative trade-off analysis is the default mode; limitations and uncertainty are acknowledged explicitly; *further research is needed* is a legitimate conclusion. No deliverable closes with humour or levity.

The standard is grounded in the voice-consistency findings of Wallace and Wray (2021), who document register drift as a recurrent pattern in long-running doctoral writing projects, and in the argumentative-structure tradition of Toulmin (2003).

## R8 — Ethics, integrity, and AI disclosure

The author is the sole intellectual author of every claim, framing, and conclusion. AI assistance is used as a research and drafting aid and is disclosed in compliance with each target journal's policy on AI-assisted writing as of the submission date. Plagiarism, undisclosed self-plagiarism, fabricated data, and unverifiable citations are absolute disqualifications. The Paper Evaluation Chamber explicitly screens for these.

The standard is grounded in the publication-ethics literature. The Committee on Publication Ethics (2019) Core Practices specify the integrity baseline; the COPE position statement on authorship and AI tools clarifies that AI tools cannot meet authorship criteria and that AI assistance must be disclosed. Recent literature on LLM-assisted scholarly writing (Lund et al., 2023; van Dis et al., 2023; Stokel-Walker & Van Noorden, 2023) sets operational expectations.

## R10 — The Paper Evaluation Chamber gate is mandatory

No paper leaves Stoa for submission, resubmission, revision response, or award entry until the Paper Evaluation Chamber chat — in which the persona is [SPECIFY THE EDITORIAL-DISCIPLINE PERSONA APPROPRIATE TO YOUR DISCIPLINE; default *a Harvard Business School Dean specialised in [your subfield] and Editor-in-Chief of a world-elite journal*] — has evaluated the paper harshly, exhaustively, and to the point, and returned a *Publish-immediately* verdict. A *Revise-and-resubmit* verdict from the Chamber is itself blocking; the paper returns to its per-paper chat, the revisions are made, and the Chamber re-evaluates from scratch. There is no override.

The standard is grounded in the empirical literature on editorial peer review (Lamont, 2009; Hirschauer, 2010; Bornmann, 2011). Lamont's ethnography established that editorial judgement emerges from structured deliberation across reviewers; the Chamber treats its evaluator persona as a deliberative aggregate rather than a single voice. Hirschauer's analysis established that the binary accept/reject is a procedural simplification the Chamber retains operationally while preserving assessment richness through narrative reasoning. Bornmann supplies the substantive review-criterion checklist.

A candid limitation: the Chamber is an editorial-discipline simulator, not a real editor; its calibration against actual editorial outcomes is unknown until a corpus of papers that have passed through the gate has accumulated decisions at target journals. Adopters who treat the Chamber as a guarantee of acceptance are misusing the tool; adopters who treat it as a discipline that raises the floor of what is submitted are using the tool as designed.

## R11 — Workflow discipline

Every chat in this project declares its role at the top of its first message via a CHAT OPENER — Pipeline Control, a specific paper number, or the Paper Evaluation Chamber — and locks into that role for the chat's lifetime. Stoa loop (discuss-phase → plan-phase → execute-phase → audit-milestone) is followed inside every per-paper chat; the durable artefacts (`{n}-CONTEXT.md`, `{n}-RESEARCH.md`, `{n}-PLAN.md`) are created before substantive drafting begins.

The standard is grounded in the recent empirical literature on context degradation in LLM-assisted writing (Lund et al., 2023; van Dis et al., 2023). Long, role-mixed conversations exhibit context-rot at rates that materially degrade output quality; role isolation per chat, plus durable artefacts that survive across chats, is the operational response.

## R12 — Programme-level coherence

Cross-paper claims, definitions, datasets, and theoretical scaffolds remain consistent across the [NUMBER] outputs of the programme. A construct defined in output *n* cannot be silently redefined in output *n + k*. The Pipeline Control chat is the custodian of this coherence; before any per-paper chat ships, it checks back here for terminological and theoretical alignment with prior outputs. The capstone (if your programme has one) integrates the programme; for it to be possible, the prior outputs must remain mutually compatible.

The standard is grounded in the cumulative-knowledge literature in management research. Bettis et al. (2016) establish that progress depends on cumulative, mutually compatible findings, and that construct drift between papers — the same word for different concepts, or different words for the same concept — is a major obstacle.

## R13 — Versioning convention

Every paper and every supporting artefact follows a single, project-wide versioning convention. Working drafts are tracked as numbered file revisions in the workspace. Each substantive revision increments the integer; minor edits do not. Submission-ready packages are tagged with a stable suffix and an ISO date. The Paper Evaluation Chamber's verdict is recorded in `STATE.md` against the specific version that was audited. Revision-and-resubmission packages are tagged against the journal's review round. Source materials, reference databases, and code or notebooks supporting a paper are version-controlled in git where practical and dated where not.

The standard adapts the spec-driven-development versioning discipline to academic publishing. The convention follows the principle in Aguinis et al. (2018) that methodological transparency includes provenance of the artefact itself: a reviewer or auditor must be able to identify *which version* of the paper is being evaluated, *which version* received which verdict, and *which version* was submitted to which venue.

## How to read these standards as an adopter

Read R1 through R13 in order, in a single sitting. The standards are not independent: R2 (theoretical originality) presupposes R3 (methodological transparency); R7 (voice discipline) operates inside R6 (prose register); R10 (audit gate) enforces compliance with R1 through R9 and is enforced by R11 (workflow discipline). The integrated discipline is what produces the framework's intended output bar; piecewise adoption produces piecewise compliance.

The standards are amendable. An adopter whose programme requires a different citation format than the default in R5, or a different audit-gate persona than the default in R10, edits the corresponding R-standard rather than abandoning the overall configuration. The integrity provisions in R8, the workflow discipline in R11, and the cross-paper coherence requirement in R12 are the load-bearing minimum that distinguishes a framework programme from a programme that has merely borrowed the framework's vocabulary.
