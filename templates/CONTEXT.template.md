## 1. Contribution claim and gap

*Decision required.* In one paragraph, articulate the gap in the existing literature that this output closes, the theoretical mechanism it proposes, and the boundary conditions of the claim. The gap must be anchored in the recent literature of the target outlet's subject category. Write the paragraph as if it is the contribution-claim paragraph of the introduction; if it does not stand on its own as such, it is not yet ready.

[POPULATED CONTRIBUTION CLAIM]

## 2. Theoretical framing

*Decision required.* Select and justify the theoretical lens. Name at least two alternative lenses you considered and why the chosen lens beats them on explanatory power for this specific output. Include three or more citations per candidate considered. The justification paragraph appears verbatim in the methods or theoretical-framework section of the manuscript.

[POPULATED THEORETICAL FRAMING]

## 3. Method and data

*Decision required.* Specify the method (case study, comparative analysis, design science research, mixed methods, systematic review, statistical analysis, ethnography, or other), the data sources, the sampling logic, the analytical procedure, the reproducibility provisions, and any ethics-committee or IRB compliance. Methods sections written without locking these decisions in CONTEXT first tend to drift; CONTEXT prevents the drift.

[POPULATED METHOD AND DATA SECTION]

## 4. Constructs and definitions

*Decision required.* Lock the operational definitions for the constructs your output uses. Cross-check each definition against any prior outputs in the programme that use the same construct (per `REQUIREMENTS.md` R12). Where a prior output used a working definition, decide whether this output inherits or refines, and document the choice.

[POPULATED CONSTRUCTS]

## 5. Outlet positioning and structure

*Decision required.* State where the output sits in the recent five-year publication record of your target outlet. Identify three to five anchor articles in the target outlet whose theoretical or methodological positioning this output extends or contests. Sketch the section structure (introduction → theoretical framework → methods → results or analysis → discussion → contributions and boundary conditions → limitations and further research). Confirm word-count target against the outlet's stated limits.

[POPULATED POSITIONING]

## 6. Cross-output coherence

*Decision required.* Identify any construct, dataset, or framing in this output that overlaps with another output in the programme. Flag overlaps to Pipeline Control before drafting. The programme-level rule (`REQUIREMENTS.md` R12) is that constructs cannot be silently redefined across outputs.

[POPULATED COHERENCE FLAGS]

## 7. Recognition positioning

*Decision required.* If this output is targeting a specific recognition — best-paper award, emerging-scholar award, doctoral committee endorsement, conference invitation — name it as the *primary* recognition target and tailor the discussion section's contribution paragraph to that recognition's evaluation rubric without compromising outlet acceptance. Reversible up to submission; record the choice and the rationale here.

[POPULATED RECOGNITION POSITIONING]

## 8. Ethics, AI disclosure, and reproducibility

*Decision required.* Confirm that the output complies with the integrity provisions in `REQUIREMENTS.md` R8. Pre-write the AI-disclosure paragraph in the format the target outlet requires as of the current date. Pre-commit to depositing supporting materials (data, code, analytical artefacts) in a citable repository at acceptance, where the discipline supports this. Confirm any ethics-committee or IRB approvals are in hand or in process.

[POPULATED ETHICS AND DISCLOSURE]

*Decision required.* Name the limitations the output will explicitly own in its discussion section. Limitations are a strength when named; the persona standard (Academic Vern, substance) requires explicit acknowledgement.
